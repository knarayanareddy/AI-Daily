# Council session: dynamic additions without mundane filler

Date: 2026-09-18

## Question put to the council

How can AI Daily feel alive and change from edition to edition without becoming a feed of routine links, decorative widgets, generic “engagement” prompts, or unsupported AI commentary?

## Research considered

The council reviewed current guidance and examples on recurring editorial formats, source transparency, data journalism, and reader participation:

- Reuters Institute, *Journalism, media, and technology trends and predictions 2026*: distinctiveness matters as generic summaries become cheap; a useful “since you last visited” treatment is valuable only when it reports a real delta.
- American Press Institute, *Improving accountability reporting*: alternative forms work when tailored to the reporting question; annotation, Q&A, timelines, and document-led treatments should clarify evidence rather than decorate it.
- Online Journalism Blog, *Data, data visualization and interactives within news*: charts, maps, timelines, and calculators are legitimate formats, but the interface must serve the editorial process and source data must remain inspectable.
- ICFJ, *Journalists creating products for transparency and trust*: participation and transparency products work when the audience contribution has a clear reporting purpose, consent boundary, and moderation path.
- Research on recurring formats and newsletter design: repeat the editorial question and reader promise, not the same empty block. A stable format should earn its place with a new, sourceable answer.

## Debate result

The council rejected additions whose only purpose is novelty, habit, or volume:

- quote of the day;
- generic “AI tool of the day” lists;
- unsourced predictions and trend scores;
- engagement polls with no reporting consequence;
- auto-generated reader comments or debates;
- daily trivia, streaks, badges, and gamified safety topics;
- “what to read next” link piles;
- charts that repeat a headline without changing understanding.

The council approved a **proof-of-value gate**. An additive may appear only when it answers a question the 25 Signals cannot answer as efficiently, has a named source or clearly labeled first-hand observation, and can be removed without breaking the canonical edition.

## Recommended additive roster

### 1. The Change Ledger
**Question:** What materially changed since the last edition?

Use only for a genuine delta: a release moved from preview to availability, a policy acquired an enforcement date, a claim was corrected, or an experiment produced a meaningful result. Show “before / now / evidence.” Do not merely repeat yesterday’s headline.

### 2. One Consequential Number
**Question:** What single measurement changes how we understand today’s signal?

Prefer one number over a dashboard. Include unit, date, denominator, source, and limitation. A chart is allowed only when the trend is the story; otherwise use a compact evidence card.

### 3. Builder Watch
**Question:** What public artifact can a technically minded reader inspect?

Require a repository, release, paper, demo, changelog, or issue thread. Report what changed and what remains unproven. Never turn it into a leaderboard or endorsement.

### 4. The Unanswered Question
**Question:** What should we still refuse to conclude?

Use when the evidence gap is consequential and specific. State what would resolve it and who could provide that evidence. This replaces false balance with an honest boundary.

### 5. Source Trail
**Question:** How did this edition get from a claim to a publishable signal?

Show a short chain of primary record, corroborating material, and unresolved conflict. This is especially useful when a story depends on a filing, benchmark, repository, or technical report that readers may want to inspect.

### 6. Decision Log
**Question:** What did the editorial process choose, exclude, or correct?

Use sparingly for a consequential inclusion, correction, source rejection, or safety decision. Include date, decision owner, evidence, and a revisit condition. It should increase accountability, not become newsroom diary content.

### 7. Safe Small Test
**Question:** What bounded, reversible observation can a reader make?

A stronger and narrower evolution of Five-Minute Experiment. Require dummy data, no credentials, no external side effects, a stop condition, and an observation that teaches something about the signal rather than about the reader.

### 8. Reader Field Note
**Question:** What grounded observation would improve the reporting?

Accept only moderated, consented submissions with a clear relationship to an existing signal. Label anecdote as anecdote. Do not solicit sensitive personal data, incident details, or private workplace information in an open form.

## Rotation model

Select at most two additions beyond the three existing showcase modules, and only when the gate is met. The edition manifest should record:

- `format` and one-sentence editorial question;
- source URLs or consent record;
- evidence posture;
- editor and moderator owner;
- privacy and safety boundary;
- expiry date or removal trigger;
- correction path;
- reason this belongs in this edition rather than a future one.

Recommended rhythm:

- **Update edition:** Change Ledger or Decision Log.
- **Measurement edition:** One Consequential Number or Source Trail.
- **Build/research edition:** Builder Watch or Safe Small Test.
- **Uncertainty edition:** Unanswered Question.
- **Community-supported edition:** Reader Field Note, only after moderation and consent.

No format is mandatory. If none passes the gate, publish only the 25 Signals and Evidence Desk. Absence is preferable to filler.

## Implementation recommendation

Do not add eight permanent blocks. Add a typed, editorially reviewed additive manifest and render only selected entries. Keep the visible Edition Guide limited to the entries actually present. Preserve the text-first fallback and canonical Read mode for every format.

The first two candidates worth implementing are **Change Ledger** and **One Consequential Number** because they are high-value, sourceable, compact, and naturally edition-dependent. Builder Watch and Unanswered Question should follow once their source and review fields are represented in the publication contract.
