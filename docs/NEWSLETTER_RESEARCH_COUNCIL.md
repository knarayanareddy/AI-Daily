# AI Daily showcase council: research and debate

**Date:** 17 September 2026  
**Decision:** build a 25-story showcase edition, but present it as a navigable editorial field rather than a flat feed.

## Inputs reviewed

The council reviewed current newsletter patterns across TLDR AI, The Rundown AI, The Batch, Import AI, Ben's Bites, Latent Space, One Useful Thing, The Neuron, and Last Week in AI. The review also considered a study of newsletter curation that found the strongest issues combine careful selection, concise summaries, commentary, varied sources, and a clear relationship with the reader.

The useful pattern is not to copy any one newsletter. Fast daily products win through compression and predictable sections; specialist products win through interpretation and point of view. AI Daily should combine both while making evidence and disagreement visible.

## Council debate

### 1. The compression editor
**Opinion:** 25 stories is too many if every item receives equal visual weight. Use one lead disturbance, four connected developments, then a scannable long tail.

### 2. The research editor
**Opinion:** Keep papers, product releases, incidents, and rumors separate. A research result and a company launch should not share the same certainty language.

### 3. The policy reporter
**Opinion:** The most discussion-worthy stories are often the consequences around a launch: labor, infrastructure, liability, evaluation, and access. Add a “question worth arguing about” to every item.

### 4. The skeptical fact-checker
**Opinion:** Do not turn a secondary report into a verified claim. Use `developing` or `corroborated`, keep the source type visible, and link the primary record when one exists.

### 5. The visual journalist
**Opinion:** Images should encode the tension or system relationship, not act as stock decoration. Reuse a coherent illustration system when publisher images are unavailable.

### 6. The community editor
**Opinion:** Discussion must be specific. “What do you think?” is weak; ask whether the reader would trade speed for oversight, open access for misuse risk, or convenience for provenance.

### 7. The accessibility editor
**Opinion:** The discussion prompt must be text-first and the field must remain optional. The 25-story list is the canonical reading surface; visualization is a second lens.

### 8. The product strategist
**Opinion:** A flat 25-item list creates fatigue. Provide editorial routes: Safety and control, Agents at work, Infrastructure and power, and The open frontier.

### 9. The builder
**Opinion:** Include enough product and open-source items to make the briefing useful tomorrow, not merely intellectually interesting today.

### 10. The international editor
**Opinion:** Do not let US labs define the entire field. Include Europe, Asia, open models, labor, energy, and public-sector consequences, and label uneven sourcing honestly.

### 11. The contrarian
**Opinion:** Every issue needs at least one story that complicates the dominant narrative, such as a safety improvement that creates access friction or a smaller model that changes deployment economics.

### 12. The reader advocate
**Opinion:** The user should be able to finish in five minutes, follow one thread for fifteen minutes, or inspect all evidence. Never make depth mandatory.

## Consensus execution plan

1. **Lead with a disturbance:** one story that changes the risk, capability, or governance picture.
2. **Show four connected signals:** stories linked by approved relationships, not inferred similarity.
3. **Offer a 25-story long tail:** grouped into four routes and filterable by category.
4. **Give every story five editorial fields:** Move, Consequence, Tension, Evidence posture, and Discussion prompt.
5. **Separate evidence levels:** verified primary record, corroborated multi-source report, and developing report.
6. **Use generated editorial illustrations locally** when publisher images cannot be reliably or permissibly embedded.
7. **Keep the Read list canonical:** images and maps enhance comprehension but never replace text.
8. **Add a “why this is here” route:** relationships should explain consequence, corroboration, dependency, contradiction, or same event.
9. **Preserve fail-closed behavior:** this is showcase data only; autonomous publication stays disabled.

## Proposed issue structure

- **The disturbance:** 1 lead item
- **The connected field:** 4 linked items
- **Safety and control:** 6 items
- **Agents at work:** 6 items
- **Infrastructure and power:** 6 items
- **Open frontier and society:** 6 items
- **One question to carry:** a closing prompt assembled from the issue's strongest unresolved tension

## Council decision

Populate the preview with 25 carefully labeled showcase stories now. Do not pretend that all 25 are equally verified. Let the interface teach the reader the difference between a primary announcement, an independently reported development, and an open question. Defer real-user testing and the production canary as requested, but do not remove those gates from the release plan.
